## Detailed Tool Comparisons

### Cerberus vs. Standalone Nmap
- **The Problem with Raw Nmap:** Nmap is the undisputed industry standard for raw network auditing, but it is a point-in-time CLI tool. It does not track device identity over time, does not recognize when a smartphone changes its IP or randomizes its MAC address, and does not provide an endpoint view.
- **The Cerberus Advantage:** Cerberus harnesses Nmap as an internal worker for Stage 3 versioning, while wrapping it in automatic scheduling, link-layer neighbor extraction, RustScan acceleration, and persistent SQLite history.

### Cerberus vs. Consumer Tools (Fing, Angry IP Scanner)
- **Consumer Limitations:** Tools like Fing or Angry IP are simple to use, but they only perform superficial ping sweeps and top-port checks. They cannot audit custom ports used by IoT backdoors, do not assess CVE vulnerabilities, and frequently rely on closed proprietary cloud backends.
- **The Cerberus Advantage:** 100% open-source, local-first data storage, full 65,535-port capability, and real CVE matching using authoritative vulnerability feeds.

### Cerberus vs. Enterprise EDR (Osquery, Falco, Wazuh)
- **EDR Limitations:** Endpoint agents provide deep visibility, but they only protect devices where you can install an agent. They have zero visibility into unauthorized smart TVs, rogue Wi-Fi extenders, or compromised unmanaged laptops on your network.
- **The Cerberus Advantage:** Unified visibility. Cerberus audits unmanaged hosts across the wire while enriching managed Linux servers with kernel eBPF observability.
