# Introduction to Cerberus

**Cerberus** is a modern, cross-platform cybersecurity intelligence platform designed to protect private local networks and Linux endpoints.

Think of your local network like an office building. Most security tools only check who comes in the front door, or only monitor people already sitting at their desks. **Cerberus does both**: it constantly maps every physical and wireless device in the building (Network Sentinel), while simultaneously placing a low-profile security guard inside Linux servers (eBPF Agent) to watch for suspicious behavior.

## Why Cerberus Was Built

Traditional network security usually requires choosing between two incomplete approaches:

1. **Active Network Scanners (like standard Nmap):** Great at probing open ports, but painfully slow when auditing all 65,535 ports. They cannot see inside machines to detect in-memory malware or stolen credentials.
2. **Endpoint Detection Agents (like Osquery or Falco):** Powerful on the machines where they are installed, but completely blind to unmanaged devices like smart TVs, IoT cameras, printers, and visiting smartphones.

Cerberus bridges this gap into a **single, unified pane of glass**:

## High-Level Benefits

### For Non-Technical Stakeholders
- **Plain-English Device Names:** Instead of confusing numbers like `192.168.1.144` or obscure MAC addresses, Cerberus identifies `"Alice's MacBook Pro (macOS)"` or `"Living Room Smart TV (LG webOS)"`.
- **Fleet Posture Rating (0–100):** A single health score representing your fleet's overall security posture. If a machine exposes a critical vulnerability or a suspicious reverse shell, the score drops immediately.
- **100% Private & Local:** All scan data, device history, and security events stay on your local machine in an embedded SQLite database. No credentials or logs are uploaded to any external cloud.

### For Security Engineers
- **Tri-Stage Hybrid Engine (`internal/scanner`):** Merges Layer-2 ARP sweeps, RustScan socket multiplexing, and targeted Nmap version audits into one non-overlapping pipeline.
- **Kernel-Level eBPF Telemetry:** Collects process executions, memory injections, and interactive commands on Linux with sub-millisecond overhead.
- **Deterministic ATT&CK Rules:** Replaces fuzzy alert noise with deterministic rules mapped to MITRE ATT&CK tactics (webshells, ptrace injection, privilege escalation).
